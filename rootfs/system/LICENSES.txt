SynthOS - third-party components and their licenses

SynthOS is written from scratch: the kernel, the boot loaders, the desktop, the
Windows subsystem, the compression, crypto and picture code are its own. The
parts below come from other projects; this file carries the notices their
licenses ask for. Read it with: cat /system/LICENSES.txt

Component            Where in SynthOS                        License
-------------------  --------------------------------------  -----------------------
Inter (fonts)        desktop fonts (kernel/gui/uifont.c,     SIL Open Font License 1.1
                     /system/fonts/ui.lz), drawn from Inter
DejaVu (fonts)       /system/fonts/DejaVuSans.ttf (subset),  Bitstream Vera license,
                     the console font, the monospace font    DejaVu changes public domain
Mozilla CA list      /etc/certs/mozilla.pem                  Mozilla Public License 2.0
NASA photographs     /system/wallpapers/*.jpg                public domain (US), see
                                                             /system/wallpapers/CREDITS.txt
shim (UEFI)          EFI partition: BOOTX64.EFI, mmx64.efi   BSD 2-clause, its notice is
                     (signed by Microsoft, from Ubuntu)      \SYNTHOS\SHIM.TXT on that partition
Chromium             desktop image only: /home/apps/chromium BSD 3-clause and the licenses
                     (unchanged 32-bit Windows snapshot)     of its parts: chrome://credits

Programs from the App Store are not part of SynthOS: the store downloads them
from their makers' own servers when you press GET, under their own licenses
(Google Chrome: the Google Chrome Terms of Service; Sysinternals tools: the
Sysinternals Software License Terms; NirSoft tools: freeware; the rest are open
source). SynthOS does not change or pass them on.

Names such as Windows, macOS, Chrome, Firefox and Claude are trademarks of their
owners. SynthOS runs programs made for Windows and offers shortcut sets like
those of Windows and macOS; it is not made, approved or sold by those owners.
